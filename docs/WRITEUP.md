# Does the scaffold change what fails? Defects in robot code at three API levels

Draft results-and-methods section for the paper (Phase 9, 2026-10-05). Every number
is regenerated by a committed script; the commands are listed under each table.

## 1. Question

At S1, open-weight models fail at robot code mostly for non-robotic reasons, and a
failing program is usually a fraction of an edit from working. S3 and S4 remove the
high-level helpers: no `get_object_pose` or `goto_pose`, only perception
(SAM3, molmo, Contact-GraspNet), `solve_ik`, `move_to_joints` and the gripper. S4
also removes the worked example. We ask whether this lower abstraction changes
*which* defects programs carry, and *how many*, across a 21-model ladder
(0.6B–32B).

## 2. Method

**Corpus.** The failing programs of the tier grid (21 models × 4 tasks × 3 tiers,
30 fixed scenes per cell, greedy decoding): 70 programs at S1 and 169 at S3/S4.

**Minimal repairs.** Each failing program is repaired with the smallest edit that
makes it work, keeping the model's own code. A repair is accepted on the bar
below. Environment workarounds that a correct program would also need are shared
as a kit and recorded per program as *substitutions*, never as defects:
- subtracting the green cube's pixels from the red grasp mask;
- one fixed IK solve to reset the solver's seed, which persists across trials;
- the wipe and placement kits.

**Acceptance bar.** A repair is accepted when no scene fails in all k draws. k is
measured per task as the smallest number of draws at which the task's own
reference program is rejected at most 5% of the time:

| task | reference (rate) | k |
|---|---|---|
| spill_wipe | REFERENCE (100%) | 1 |
| cube_lifting | LIFT template (86.8%) | 5 |
| cube_stack, point-prompt programs | v7pg (66.9%) | 12 |
| cube_stack, text-prompt programs | v7pg_text (60.2%) | 16 |
| nut_assembly | v8 (87/90; fails scene 14) | 8 |

cube_stack is judged per perception route, because the route is the model's
choice and the text route loses one scene (73%) far more often than the point
route (20%). (`scripts/verify_reduced_repair.py`, `kref` subcommand)

**Tags.** Every program is tagged by hand against its verified repair: one tag per
distinct cause the repair had to fix, in the S1 taxonomy (`opencap/editclass.FAMILY`,
six families). S1 was originally tagged by an automated classifier. On the same 70
S1 programs, hand tagging finds 2.77 tags per program against the classifier's
2.06, with low agreement (Jaccard 0.31 on a blind sample). So all S1 programs were
re-tagged by hand, and every comparison below is hand against hand. The automated
S1 counts are reported alongside.

**Coverage.** All 169 S3/S4 programs are tagged:
- 114 verified minimal repairs;
- 2 programs that pass as written;
- 36 nut programs tagged against the shared reference, whose own failure on
  scene 14 means they cannot be accepted ("not verified");
- 2 repairs rejected on the bar (one scene failing every draw, no
  model-attributable cause);
- 15 programs that end mid-token, tagged `syntax` only and excluded from the
  counts.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 What fails changes

| family | S1 (n=70) | S3/S4 (n=154) | verified only (n=116) | Fisher p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FRAME | 4% [1, 12] | 47% [40, 55] | 48% | ≈1e-11 |
| INTERFACE | 26% [17, 37] | 60% [52, 67] | 52% | ≈3e-6 |
| TRAJECTORY | 47% [36, 59] | 19% [14, 26] | 23% | ≈5e-5 |
| STRUCTURE | 29% [19, 40] | 35% [28, 43] | 33% | n.s. |
| SEMANTIC | 94% [86, 98] | 95% [90, 97] | 93% | 1.0 |

Share of failing programs with at least one tag in the family; Wilson 95%
intervals. (`python3 scripts/cross_tier_report.py --no-truncated [--verified-only]`)

- **FRAME** errors are the signature of the reduced API: camera-frame values
  used as world coordinates, wxyz/xyzw mix-ups, and a 4×4 transform applied to a
  3-vector. At S1 the API returns world poses and these almost never happen.
  FRAME rises on every task: cube_lifting 0 → 37%, cube_stack 0 → 50%,
  nut_assembly 14 → 44%, spill_wipe 0 → 59%.
- **INTERFACE** errors (misread returns and arguments, wrong prompts, unbound
  names) also rise on every task.
- **TRAJECTORY** falls because S1's `goto_pose(z_approach=…)` argument, the
  source of most S1 TRAJECTORY tags, does not exist in the reduced API. That
  reflects the API surface, not model behaviour.
- **SEMANTIC** defects (wrong target, wrong formula) are near-universal at every
  tier. An earlier report of a rise came from comparing the automated S1 tagger
  with hand tags, and is retracted.

### 3.2 How much fails changes, and the scale advantage disappears

| | ≤2B | 3–4B | 7–9B | ≥12B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | 4.17 | 2.70 | 1.54 | 1.54 |
| S3 | 4.10 | 4.61 | 3.53 | 3.46 |
| S4 | 3.20 | 3.82 | 3.56 | 3.21 |

Mean defect tags per failing program. With the automated S1 tagger the S1 row
reads 3.21 → 1.15. (`python3 scripts/cross_tier_report.py --by-band --no-truncated`)

- At S1, defects per failing program fall with scale. At S3/S4 they sit
  between 3.2 and 4.6 in every band.
- The ≥12B models' failing programs carry about twice their S1 count: 1.54
  against 3.33 (verified only 3.03), Mann-Whitney p = 0.0002.
- Per model, this holds for **every model of 3B and up (15 of 15; sign test
  p ≈ 3e-5)**. The five ≤2B models are already at 3–5 defects per program at S1
  and do not rise.
- FRAME defects occur for 19 of 21 models at S3/S4, against 3 of 21 at S1. The
  two exceptions are the smallest models, whose programs fail before reaching any
  coordinate math. (`python3 scripts/per_model_profile.py`)

### 3.3 Checks

- **Token cap.** Raising the cap from 2048 to 8192 tokens does not explain any
  ≥12B failure:
  - qwen3vl-8b and qwen3vl-32b loop on one line at any budget;
  - gemma3-12b completes at 2,132 tokens and still crashes at line 27;
  - of 50 truncated programs, 35 already carry another diagnosed defect.
- **Privileged state.** The observation also exposes object poses. A scan of all
  5,040 S3/S4 episodes found no program reading them.
- **Reliability.** Two checks, both on the same 60-program sample (30 per side).

  *Same rater, blind re-tag* (Claude Opus 5, the rater that produced the filed
  tags): pooled κ 0.89, FRAME 0.67, INTERFACE 0.90. An upper bound, not
  inter-rater agreement.

  *Independent rater* (GPT-6 Astra, a different model family, blind to the
  question and isolated from the repository): the contrasts reproduce in its
  own labels — FRAME 2/30 → 14/30 against the filed 1/30 → 13/30, INTERFACE
  11/30 → 18/30 against 10/30 → 17/30. Per-family κ against the filed tags is
  **FRAME 0.82** and **INTERFACE 0.87**; pooled κ 0.72, mean Jaccard 0.67.
  FRAME agrees *better* across raters than the same rater agreed with itself.

  Two low κ values are marginal artifacts rather than disagreement: SEMANTIC
  0.40 sits at 92% raw agreement with 53 of 60 programs tagged by both, and
  SYNTAX has n=3. One is real: TRAJECTORY, 68% raw agreement with 16
  rater-only cells, discussed in §4.

  The count gap moves under every pass — filed +0.63, same-rater re-tag +0.13,
  independent rater +0.33 — same sign, three magnitudes. **It is the least
  robust result and the family contrasts should be quoted ahead of it.** The
  count-free form in §3.2 is not subject to it.

## 4. Limitations

- **One rater tagged both sides, and both raters are language models.** The
  filed tags come from Claude Opus 5 in an agent session; the `tagged_by` field
  reads "hand (CLI session)", which distinguishes a reading pass from the
  automated classifier, not a person from a machine. The independent check is
  GPT-6 Astra. No human has rated the packet; `artifacts/second_rater/` is
  ready for one and is scored by the same command. Note also the asymmetry: the
  filed rater knew the research question and the independent one did not.

- **The two raters draw one boundary differently, and it is a boundary in the
  taxonomy.** The independent rater used `approach_param` 31 times against the
  filed 13. Where a near-total rewrite supplies an entire motion sequence that
  includes an approach offset, the filed tags record one cause
  (`missing_action` — the task's final step was absent) and the independent
  rater records two. Both see the same defect and differ on how finely to split
  it. Family *presence* is unaffected, which is why FRAME and INTERFACE
  reproduce; TRAJECTORY's *level* is rater-dependent (S1 reads 43% filed
  against 80% independent) while its *direction* is not — both find it falls at
  S3/S4. Since that fall also follows from the API surface (§3.1), the
  direction is what we claim.
- These are failing programs only. The counts describe what goes wrong, not how
  often programs fail.
- The nut S3/S4 records rest on a reference that fails one scene. They are
  excluded from the verified-only figures, and the findings hold without them.
- The text-prompt route was given its own k. A reviewer could argue for a single
  k per task. Under k=12, one more cube_stack repair (b9a6c83ddbc4, accepted at
  draw 16) would have been rejected. Its tags would stay, filed as not accepted.
